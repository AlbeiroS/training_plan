How to run each session

Slow day · Easy

Comfortable, conversational, unhurried. This is the aerobic base — you build the engine here without burning matches. Run it slower than feels natural.

Test: you should be able to speak full sentences without gasping.

Interval day

Always: 1 mile warm-up, then the reps with a 200 m easy jog between each, then 1 mile cool-down. Reps are run hard but controlled — every rep should be roughly the same time. If the last one is much slower than the first, you started too fast.

Test: you can say 2 or 3 words at the end of a rep, and you recover enough in the 200 m jog to hit the next one.

Long run

Slower than your slow day. The goal is time on feet, not pace. This is where you train your head as much as your legs.

Practice: from week 5 on, rehearse hydration and a gel or snack every 45 minutes.

5 rules for this plan

  • Leave at least one full day between sessions. Any three days that work for you are fine — just keep the order: slow, intervals, long.
  • About 80% of your volume should feel easy. Only the interval reps are meant to be hard.
  • Weeks 4, 7 and 10 drop on purpose. Don't skip ahead or add miles — adaptation happens in the down weeks.
  • If a niggle changes how you run, stop. Losing three days now beats losing race day.
  • Nothing new on race day: no new shoes, clothes, food or gel you haven't tested on a long run.
